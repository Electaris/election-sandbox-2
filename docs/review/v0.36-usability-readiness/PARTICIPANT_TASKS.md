# American Electorate — task cards

Give one card at a time. Do not send the moderator guide or show later tasks in advance. These are prepared prompts; no participant sessions have been conducted.

## Opening

We are testing the product, not you. Please say what you expect to happen and what the numbers mean as you work. Some things may be unclear; that is useful feedback. You may stop at any time. Please do not enter personal information.

## First impression

Spend a minute exploring the opening page. What do you think this product lets you do? What would you try first?

## Task 1 — Change an outcome

Using American Electorate, make Harris win Pennsylvania. Then explain which geographic areas contributed most to the change.

## Task 2 — Stop short

Improve Harris's Pennsylvania result but do not let Harris win the state. Determine exactly how much additional movement would still be required.

## Task 3 — Build a route

Find the path to 270 requiring the fewest net margin votes and begin constructing that route using any detailed state the product supports.

## Task 4 — Return to your work

Save or copy your scenario, leave the application, reopen it, and determine whether the same election was restored.

## Task 5 — Explain the election

Explain why the Electoral College result differs from the certified 2024 result. Identify the states and assumptions responsible.

## Task 6 — Explore election night

Watch your scenario unfold as election night. Pause the count, examine an earlier moment, and return to editing your scenario. Explain what the replay does and does not tell you about the real election.

## Task 7 — Assess a demographic assumption

Explore a turnout change for a population group in Pennsylvania or Michigan. Explain where the candidate choices for added ballots come from. Then determine whether you could make the same kind of group change in Wisconsin and why.

## After each task

How confident are you that your explanation is correct, from 1 to 5? What made you choose that rating?

## Closing

What was the hardest part? Which result would you feel comfortable sharing, and what context would you include? What would stop you from using this again?
